# Asset Management and Facility Operations

## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes standards for managing company assets, protecting facilities, and ensuring that physical resources are used efficiently and securely. It covers office equipment, furniture, tools, vehicles, inventory, and other corporate property. The goal is to reduce loss, support accountability, and maintain operational continuity.

## 2. Asset Ownership and Responsibility

All company assets must be assigned to an owner, department, or individual responsible for custody, maintenance, and appropriate use. Asset ownership must be clearly documented in approved inventory systems or departmental records. Managers are accountable for verifying that assigned assets are used for business purposes and are not lost, damaged, or retained unnecessarily.

Assets include company-owned equipment, leased equipment, software subscriptions, intellectual property, and physical inventories where applicable. Employees must treat these resources as valuable business assets and must not use them for personal gain or unauthorized activities.

## 3. Acquisition, Tracking, and Tagging

New assets must be acquired through approved purchasing and receiving processes. Equipment or inventory should be tagged or recorded in the asset register before being placed into service. Asset records should include asset type, location, custodian, acquisition date, value, serial number or identifier, and disposition status.

Any transfer of custody must be documented. When equipment is moved between sites or departments, the relevant asset owner and the operations or facilities team should update the asset register promptly.

## 4. Maintenance and Inspection

Assets must be maintained in safe working condition and used according to manufacturer guidance and safety procedures. Equipment that is damaged, outdated, unsafe, or no longer fit for purpose should be removed from service or repaired as appropriate.

Routine inspections should be performed for facilities, fire safety equipment, office furniture, vehicles, and other relevant resources. Defects that pose a risk to safety or operational continuity should be reported without delay and addressed in a timely manner.

## 5. Facility Access and Security

Facility access is controlled to protect employees, visitors, and property. Staff must not share access cards, bypass alarms, or leave doors unlocked when access control requires otherwise. Visitors and contractors must follow site procedures, remain in authorized areas, and receive escort support when required.

Managers must ensure that workspaces are secure, well organized, and maintained to reduce hazards. Doors, keys, windows, alarm systems, and emergency exit routes must remain in functional order.

## 6. Stock, Storage, and Inventory Controls

Inventory and storage areas must be kept in an orderly condition and protected against loss, accidental damage, or unauthorized use. Reorder levels, stock counts, and storage conditions should be managed according to operational need and local safety requirements. Materials that may pose a fire, chemical, or contamination risk should be managed with the appropriate handling controls.

Periodic inventory checks should be performed to validate the actual stock against records. Variances must be investigated promptly, and corrective actions should be documented.

## 7. Use and Care Standards

Employees are expected to use company assets responsibly and according to the purpose for which they were assigned. Reasonable wear and tear is expected, but deliberate misuse, negligence, theft, or negligent damage is prohibited. Employees are responsible for reporting damage, malfunction, or loss as soon as they become aware of it.

Use of company vehicles, tools, or specialized equipment requires authorization, training, and compliance with safety policies. Restricted or hazardous equipment may only be used by trained individuals.

## 8. Disposal and Surplus Management

When an asset reaches the end of useful life, is obsolete, is damaged beyond repair, or is no longer needed, it must be disposed of or redeployed through approved procedures. Disposal may require environmental, finance, or legal review depending on the asset type and value.

Sensitive information stored on devices or equipment must be securely wiped before disposal or reassignment. Physical records and media should be destroyed according to retention and confidentiality requirements.

## 9. Insurance, Incident Response, and Recovery

Assets should be protected against risk through insurance, safeguarding procedures, and contingency planning where appropriate. Loss or damage of significant assets may require immediate reporting to facilities, finance, security, or insurance contacts.

In the event of a major facility or asset incident, the company may need to isolate affected areas, secure property, and initiate business continuity procedures. Accurate records of the incident and the recovery steps are required for operational learning and possible claims.

## 10. Training and Compliance

Employees with asset handling, facilities, or inventory responsibilities must be trained on the relevant procedures, documentation standards, and safety requirements. Managers should reinforce asset stewardship principles and ensure that assigned custodians understand their obligations.

Failure to protect or track company assets may result in corrective action, reimbursement obligations, or disciplinary action. The company may also recover costs associated with lost, misused, or damaged assets.
