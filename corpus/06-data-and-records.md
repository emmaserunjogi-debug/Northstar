# DAT-06: Privacy, Information Handling, and Records

**Company:** Northstar Cloud Services (fictional)  
**Owner:** Privacy Lead  
**Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** 13 October 2026 | **Review due:** 13 October 2027  
**Classification:** Internal | **Status:** Synthetic example policy

## 1. Classification and responsible collection

Classification scheme
Public information is approved for external release. Internal information includes routine procedures and nonpublic operating notes. Confidential information includes contracts, nonpublic finances, and ordinary customer business data. Restricted information includes authentication secrets, sensitive personal information, and high-impact customer datasets. The information owner assigns the classification; mixed collections follow the highest applicable level.

Handling rules
Public information may be shared freely after publication approval. Internal information stays within approved worker systems. Confidential information requires named recipients, approved encrypted storage and transfer, and a valid business purpose. Restricted information also requires explicit owner approval for external disclosure, tightly limited access, and an approved secure transfer method. Do not send secrets as ordinary attachments.

Collection and use
Collect only information needed for a stated, authorized purpose. The Privacy Lead reviews new personal-data uses before collection begins, including notices, appropriate grounds, recipient access, storage location, and deletion plans. Do not reuse customer information for marketing, analytics, or model training merely because it is technically accessible.

Accountability
Each business dataset has a named owner, a purpose, classification, storage location, and retention category in the records inventory. Owners correct misclassification promptly and consult the Privacy Lead when a use or sharing request is unclear. SEC-05 governs technical controls; FIN-07 governs new suppliers.

## 2. Individual requests, sharing, and suspected breaches

Request intake
Forward requests to access, correct, delete, or restrict personal information to the Privacy Lead within one business day. Record receipt time, contact details, request scope, and the system involved in the restricted privacy queue. Do not promise completion or disclose records before identity and authority are verified using a proportionate method.

Assessment and response
The Privacy Lead records the applicable legal or contractual deadline and assigns internal tasks within two business days. Owners locate relevant records and identify exclusions, holds, third-party information, and technical limitations. The Privacy Lead approves the response and records what was supplied, changed, withheld, or retained and why. Internal targets never extend an applicable external deadline.

External sharing
Before a new disclosure, confirm purpose, recipient identity, minimum required fields, contract terms, security, and any location restrictions. The Privacy Lead reviews personal-data transfers and Finance retains approved vendor terms under FIN-07. For one-off approved sharing, the owner records recipient, date, content category, and transfer method.

Suspected breach
A misdirected email, exposed folder, lost record, or unauthorized lookup must be reported immediately under INC-09. Attempt safe containment, such as revoking a sharing link, while preserving evidence. The Privacy Lead determines notification obligations with qualified advice. Workers must not independently notify all customers or claim that a breach is harmless.

## 3. Retention schedule, holds, and disposal

Default internal schedule
Policy versions, approvals, and exceptions: three years after supersession or closure. Conduct cases and safety reports: three years after closure. Employment records: three years after departure. Financial transactions, expenses, and supplier contracts: seven years after transaction or contract end, whichever applies. Customer account records: two years after account closure. Support tickets: two years after closure. Incident records: three years after closure. Continuity plans and exercise records: three years after supersession or exercise. Access requests and reviews: one year after closure. Security logs: 90 days from creation. Unsuccessful applicant records: six months after the hiring decision.

Customer content and backups
Customer-hosted content follows the signed contract's retention and deletion terms. If terms are absent, the Privacy Lead must resolve them before ingestion. Operational backups follow BCP-10's 35-day rolling window and are not a long-term archive. Deleted material may remain in protected backups until rotation; if restored, required deletions must be reapplied before normal use.

Overrides and holds
These are illustrative company defaults, not statements of legal minimums. The Privacy Lead records approved jurisdictional or contractual overrides. A legal, dispute, or investigation hold suspends deletion of affected material until authorized release; record scope, custodian, and release decision.

Disposal procedure
Owners review eligible records monthly, check holds and overrides, and authorize deletion. IT or the records custodian performs secure deletion or approved physical destruction. The disposal log identifies category, date range, method, authorizer, and completion date without copying the deleted content.
