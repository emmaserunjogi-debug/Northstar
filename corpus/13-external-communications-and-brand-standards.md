# External Communications and Brand Standards

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets expectations for how employees and teams communicate with customers, suppliers, regulators, media, community stakeholders, and the public. It protects the company’s reputation, ensures consistent messaging, and supports lawful, professional communication across all channels.

## 2. Communication Authority

Only authorized persons may speak on behalf of the company in official external communications. Managers, designated spokespersons, and communications representatives must coordinate with Legal and Marketing or Communications where needed. This requirement is especially important in customer incidents, legal inquiries, media requests, regulatory interactions, and product or service commitments.

Employees who are not designated spokespeople should avoid making public statements that could be interpreted as official company positions. If asked to comment publicly, employees should redirect the question to the appropriate communications lead.

## 3. Brand and Message Consistency

The company’s brand is built on trust, reliability, and clear professional communication. External messages should be accurate, aligned with approved materials, and consistent with company values. Content should be factual, respectful, and tailored to the audience while avoiding exaggeration, misleading statements, or inconsistent guarantees.

Approved brand guidelines should be used for logos, slogans, templates, email signatures, presentation materials, product references, and collateral. Any mention of the company in external content should be reviewed when the content affects risk, customer commitments, or public perception.

## 4. Customer and Vendor Communication

Employees communicating with customers, partners, or suppliers should use professional language, truthful descriptions, and agreed service levels. Communications must be consistent with contractual obligations, approved pricing, delivery windows, and work commitments. Employees should not make commitments that are not authorized or that may be inconsistent with internal process.

When a customer complaint, performance concern, or service issue arises, the responsible manager should coordinate a consistent response and, where appropriate, escalate to sales, operations, customer success, or Legal. The company should not misrepresent the status of an issue or conceal known material risks.

## 5. Crisis, Media, and Regulatory Communications

During a crisis, incident, or significant disruption, communications must be timely, coordinated, and factual. Leadership and communications teams will establish the message and the communication flow. Employees with relevant information should share facts promptly and avoid creating rumor or speculation.

All media inquiries, statements to regulators, law enforcement, or public authorities should be routed through the authorized communications or legal channels. Employees must not independently promise a response, settlement, or regulatory accommodation unless authorized to do so.

## 6. Social Media and Public Platforms

Use of social media and public digital channels must align with this policy and with unacceptable conduct policies. Employees may not publish or repost information that is defamatory, discriminatory, confidential, or otherwise harmful. Personal social media activity may be subject to policy when it relates to the company, impacts the workplace, or reveals company information.

Marketing and communications staff should use approved hashtags, campaign guidelines, and messaging tools. Employees acting in external-facing roles should not provide commentary that could be mistaken for official company policy or product positioning unless authorized.

## 7. Accessibility, Inclusion, and Respectful Language

External communications should be accessible, inclusive, and respectful. The company should use clear language, avoid exclusionary or insensitive phrasing, and make reasonable efforts to accommodate accessibility needs where relevant. This includes documents, websites, customer-facing materials, and public statements.

Communications that involve individuals or communities should avoid stereotyping, bias, or assumptions. The company values clarity, professionalism, and fairness in all external engagement.

## 8. Records and Approval Requirements

External communication materials should be retained in the company records repository when they are business-relevant or may support legal, audit, or operational review. Drafts, approvals, announcements, presentations, and customer communications should be maintained according to the records retention schedule.

Where a communication has legal, operational, or financial implications, it should be reviewed and approved in accordance with the document control and approval framework. The company should not rely on informal approval for formal statements, customer commitments, or public-facing announcements.

## 9. Reporting and Escalation

Employees who are uncertain about the appropriate communication channel or who become aware of a misleading or unauthorized statement should escalate the matter to the communications lead, Legal, or a manager. Concern about public reputational risk, customer harm, or legal exposure should be reported immediately.

The company supports open reporting and prohibits retaliation against employees who raise concerns about external communication risks in good faith.

## 10. Review and Continuous Improvement

This policy will be reviewed periodically to reflect customer needs, market conditions, communication practices, and legal or regulatory changes. The company will use lessons learned from customer interactions, incidents, and formal reviews to improve communication quality and brand consistency.
