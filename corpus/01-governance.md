# GOV-01: Company Handbook and Policy Governance

**Company:** Northstar Cloud Services (fictional)  
**Owner:** Chief Operating Officer  
**Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** 13 October 2026 | **Review due:** 13 October 2027  
**Classification:** Internal | **Status:** Synthetic example policy

## 1. Company profile and policy framework

Purpose and scope
Northstar Cloud Services is a fictional, 45-person business that provides hosted workflow software and implementation services to business customers. It has one shared office and a distributed workforce. This corpus is synthetic training and demonstration material; the approvals, company, and procedures are illustrative, not evidence of an operating organization or legal compliance.

Policy baseline
These policies apply to employees, temporary workers, and contractors wherever relevant to their access or duties. All ten documents are version 1.0, effective 13 October 2026, with review due 13 October 2027. Their classification is Internal. Applicable law and signed contractual commitments take precedence; the COO resolves internal conflicts with qualified advice where necessary. Workers must report a conflict rather than silently disregard a requirement.

Operating conventions
A business day is Monday through Friday excluding company-observed holidays. Business hours are 09:00-17:00 East Africa Time. Deadlines in hours are elapsed hours unless explicitly described as business hours. Emergency response and security reporting operate at all times. Financial thresholds are in USD; Finance records the conversion rate for other currencies on the approval date.

Where work is recorded
The Service Desk holds access, procurement, support, and incident tickets with restricted queues where needed. The People Register holds employment records. The Policy Register holds controlled documents and exceptions. The Vendor Register holds suppliers and contracts. These are conceptual systems, not live links or accounts.

## 2. Decision rights and document control

Accountability
The CEO approves company policies and spending above $5,000. The COO owns this framework, continuity, and cross-functional disputes. People Operations owns employment administration and conduct case handling. The IT Lead owns access, device security, and technical incident response. The Privacy Lead owns personal-data decisions. Finance owns payment controls. The Support Lead owns customer case operations. A designated board representative handles concerns involving the CEO.

Approval rules
An approver cannot approve their own expense, access, exception, or conduct case. The next uninvolved leader acts instead; the board representative acts for the CEO. Delegation must be written, time-limited, recorded in the Policy Register, and limited to the delegator's authority. Delegation never permits self-approval or bypasses Finance's independent payment verification.

Document change procedure
The owner opens a change entry with the reason, affected documents, proposed wording, and operational impact. Relevant owners review cross-references and thresholds. The COO checks consistency and the CEO approves. The owner publishes the dated version, archives the superseded copy, and assigns training before any new control takes effect. Emergency interim instructions require CEO approval and an expiry within 30 days.

Worker access
People Operations gives each worker the applicable policies at onboarding and records acknowledgment within five business days. Acknowledgment confirms receipt, not waiver of any right. Owners keep an accessible current copy and explain changes to affected teams.

## 3. Exceptions, assurance, and corpus map

Exception procedure
Before deviating, the requester records the policy clause, business reason, risks, compensating controls, owner, and proposed expiry in the Policy Register. The policy owner and COO must approve; the CEO handles requests involving the COO. Exceptions last no more than 90 days and require fresh review to renew. No exception can authorize unlawful conduct, retaliation, falsification, or a contractual breach. An imminent safety emergency is handled immediately and documented afterward.

Assurance and corrective action
Owners review open exceptions monthly and sample compliance quarterly. Each finding has evidence, an accountable person, a due date, and closure verification by someone other than the implementer. The COO reviews overdue high-risk findings each week. Policy violations receive proportionate review under ETH-02, with an opportunity to respond; discipline is not automatic.

Corpus map
GOV-01 defines common rules. ETH-02 covers conduct and reporting. PPL-03 covers joining, leave, changes, and departure. WRK-04 covers remote work and safety. SEC-05 covers accounts and technology. DAT-06 covers information handling and retention. FIN-07 covers procurement and expenses. SUP-08 covers customer support and releases. INC-09 covers incidents and communication. BCP-10 covers continuity and recovery.

Evidence and retention
The Policy Register preserves approvals, version history, exceptions, and assurance findings for three years after supersession or closure under DAT-06. Every quarterly review records the sample, result, and follow-up. A reported weakness is treated as an improvement opportunity unless evidence warrants a conduct investigation.
