# Acceptable Use and Digital Communications

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the acceptable use of company systems, devices, networks, and digital communication tools. It supports productivity, security, and lawful conduct while preventing misuse of company resources. The company provides technology to support business operations, and every employee is expected to use it responsibly and in accordance with applicable policy.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees, contractors, interns, temporary workers, and volunteers who use company-provided technology or company data in connection with their work. It also applies to communications sent from personal devices when those communications are used for company business or include company information.

The policy covers email, messaging platforms, file-sharing systems, video conferencing, cloud services, collaboration tools, social media, and any other digital channels used in conducting company business.

## 3. Business Use Only

Company technology must be used for legitimate business purposes. Personal use may be permitted if it is minimal, non-disruptive, and does not interfere with work obligations, expose the company to risk, or violate policy. Employees should not use company resources for personal commercial activity, political campaigning, or illegal conduct.

Use of company technology to access or distribute inappropriate content, hateful or discriminatory material, content that violates employment policies, or any online material that could damage the company’s reputation or operations is prohibited.

## 4. Security and System Integrity

Employees must not disable, bypass, or circumvent security controls, network protections, or monitoring tools. Attempts to hack, exploit, or test system vulnerabilities without authorization are prohibited. Software installations, browser extensions, scripts, and plug-ins may only be used when approved and supported by IT.

Employees must use multi-factor authentication where required, protect credentials, keep devices patched, and report suspicious activity promptly. Loss, theft, or suspected compromise of a company device or account must be reported immediately through the IT help desk or designated security channel.

## 5. Data Handling and Sharing

Digital communications must be appropriate to the sensitivity of the information being transmitted. Information classification requirements apply to electronic communications just as they apply to paper records. Employees must avoid sending confidential or restricted information to unapproved recipients or storing it in unsanctioned tools.

Sensitive information should be shared only through approved platforms, with access restricted to those who require it for business purposes. Employees should verify recipients before sending data and should not forward or re-share content without a valid business reason.

## 6. Email and Messaging Standards

Email and chat systems are business tools and should be used in a professional and courteous manner. Employees must preserve the integrity of company communications, avoid slang or hostile tone, and ensure that messages are clear, factual, and properly attributed.

Announcements, client communications, and decision records should be retained in approved systems when they might be needed for accountability, legal review, or operational continuity. Employees should avoid sending sensitive information via public or personal systems and should not use email to bypass formal recordkeeping controls.

## 7. Remote Work and Device Security

Employees working remotely must connect through approved channels and take reasonable steps to protect company information. Use of public Wi-Fi is permitted only if the connection is secure and the device complies with company security requirements. Screens should lock when unattended, and company devices should remain encrypted and up to date.

Staff must be vigilant against phishing and social engineering attempts. Unexpected requests for login information, payment actions, or sensitive data should be verified through an independent channel before action is taken.

## 8. Social Media and External Communications

Employees may use social media for work-related purposes only when authorized and in accordance with company branding and communication standards. Personal or unofficial use of social media must not imply endorsement by the company and must not disclose confidential information.

Employees should avoid public commentary that could damage the company’s reputation, create legal exposure, or undermine internal trust. If a question or issue involves customer, regulatory, or reputational risk, it should be escalated to the appropriate communications or legal contact.

## 9. Monitoring and Privacy

The company may monitor network usage, log activity, and review electronic communications as part of lawful business operations, security management, and compliance obligations. Monitoring is limited to the extent necessary to protect company systems, maintain productivity, and investigate potential misconduct or policy violations.

Employees should understand that email, messaging, and work-related digital content may be subject to legal hold, audit, or investigative review, and should preserve records accordingly. This does not authorize unfettered monitoring of personal accounts; the company will act in a manner consistent with law and policy.

## 10. Enforcement and Consequences

Failure to comply with this policy may result in corrective action, restriction of system access, or termination of employment or contract depending on the severity of the issue. Where misuse creates a legal, security, financial, or reputational risk, the company may initiate formal investigation, notify regulators, or take further action as appropriate.

The company expects employees to report actual or suspected misuse, policy violations, or security risks promptly. A good-faith report will not be treated as misconduct.

## 11. Review and Administration

This policy will be reviewed annually or sooner if technology, risk profiles, or legal obligations change. Questions should be directed to the IT Security team, HR, or Legal. The company may update this policy to address emerging threats, compliance changes, or operational realities.
